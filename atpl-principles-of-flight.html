Notes preview · Stall, Spin and Contamination

Notes that show the working

  • Stall: a wing stalls when it exceeds the critical angle of attack, at any speed or attitude.
  • Stall speed: Vs ∝ √weight and ∝ √n. It rises with a forward CG, ice and, at high altitude, compressibility.
  • Swept wing: the tips stall first, the CP moves forward and the nose pitches up. A straight wing stalls root-first and the nose drops.
  • Warning and protection: the stick shaker warns; the stick pusher fires at a higher angle of attack to stop it rising further.
Worked example · stall speed in a turnVs = 100 kt in level flight. Level turn at 60° bank.1. n = 1 ÷ cos 60° = 22. Vs in the turn = 100 × √2Vs ≈ 141 kt

About ATPL Principles of Flight

How many questions are in the exam, and what is the pass mark?

Under the East African CASSOA system, each ATPL theory paper is usually 40 questions in about one hour. The pass mark is 70%, but aim for 75% or more in practice so you have a margin on exam day.

Which Principles of Flight topics are tested most?

High-speed flight is the biggest chapter, followed by drag, flight mechanics (climbs, descents and turns), loads and gusts, and stability. Learn the square-root rules for stall speed and load factor until they are automatic.

Are the calculations explained?

Yes. Every calculation question, from stall speed and load factor to turn radius, climb gradient and VA, has been recomputed and comes with the working.
