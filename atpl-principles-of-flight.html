Notes preview · Limitations, Loads and Gusts

Notes that show the working

  • Load factor: n = lift ÷ weight. It is above 1 in turns, pull-ups and up-gusts, and below 1 in steady climbs and descents (n = cos γ) and push-overs.
  • Limit and ultimate load: ultimate = 1.5 × limit. Above limit the structure may deform permanently; above ultimate it fails.
  • Manoeuvring speed: VA = VS × √n limit. At or below VA, full up-elevator stalls the wing before the limit load is reached.
  • Gust load factor: Δn rises with speed, with air density and with lift-curve slope, and falls with wing loading.
Worked example · gust load factorCL 0.42, lift-curve slope 0.1 per degree, gust +3°.n = (0.42 + 0.3) ÷ 0.42n = 1.71
Worked example · manoeuvring speedVS 70 kt, n limit 2.5.VA = 70 × √2.5 = 111 kt

About ATPL Principles of Flight

How many questions are in the exam, and what is the pass mark?

Under the East African CASSOA system, each ATPL theory paper is usually 40 questions in about one hour. The pass mark is 70%, but aim for 75% or more in practice so you have a margin on exam day.

Which Principles of Flight topics are tested most?

High-speed flight, drag, climb, descent and turns, and loads and gusts make up about half the paper. Stability, propellers, flight controls and lift add most of the rest.

Does it explain why an answer is right?

Yes. Every question has an explanation that gives the reasoning, not just the key, and the calculation chapters have worked examples you can follow step by step.
