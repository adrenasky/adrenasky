Notes preview · Respiration, Circulation and Hypoxia

Notes that show the working

  • Breathing control: rate and depth are driven by CO2 in the blood, not oxygen.
  • Hypoxia types: hypoxic (low ppO2 at altitude), anaemic (CO, anaemia), stagnant (poor circulation), histotoxic (poisoned cells).
  • Thresholds: indifferent zone to 10,000 ft; short-term memory suffers from 12,000 ft; critical threshold about 20,000 ft.
  • Night vision: is affected first, from about 5,000 ft.
Key numbers · time of useful consciousness25,000 ft: 3–5 minutes30,000 ft: 45–90 seconds35,000 ft: 30–60 seconds40,000 ft and above: about 15 seconds or less
Key numbers · carbon monoxideBinds haemoglobin 210–250 times more readily than oxygen.Treat with 100% oxygen; recovery takes days.

About ATPL Human Performance

How many questions are in the exam, and what is the pass mark?

Under the East African CASSOA system, each ATPL theory paper is usually 40 questions in about one hour. The pass mark is 70%, but aim for 75% or more in practice so you have a margin on exam day.

Which Human Performance topics are tested most?

Respiration and hypoxia alone is about a fifth of the paper. Stress and sleep, CRM, and memory and attention follow. Learn the altitude thresholds and time of useful consciousness figures exactly.

Is Human Performance an easy pass?

It has less maths than other subjects, but the questions turn on precise wording and numbers. Practising with explanations is the quickest way to learn how the examiner phrases things.
